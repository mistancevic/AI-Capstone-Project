# Demo Day — format and judges' rubric

Read from the shared agenda sheet, 2026-08-07. **Caveat on provenance:** the
sheet is headed *AI Demo Day | Sunday, July 20, 2025*, and its results table
lists a previous cohort's group projects (ChatGPT, Amazon, Airbnb, Netflix,
Spotify, Google Maps). Treat it as how these judges score, not as a guarantee
of this cohort's exact format.

## Format, per presenter

| | |
|---|---|
| Presentation | 5 minutes |
| Judges Q&A | 5 minutes |
| Judges document feedback | 5 minutes |
| **Total slot** | **15 minutes** |

Five presenters, opening remarks by Moe Ali, judges deliberate, one winner
announced.

## Scoring — 15 points, three equal categories

**(1) Problem & Discovery — 5 points.** Did they clearly articulate the user
problem and its significance? How effectively did Discovery gain insight into
market, business and user context? Is the AI solution hypothesis clear,
compelling, and justified by the discovery findings?

**(2) AI Solution & Evaluation — 5 points.** How clearly and effectively did
they **demonstrate the LLM-powered prototype**? Did they **specify which
evaluation framework** they used to assess the quality and accuracy of the AI's
output? Did they **show results or evidence from the development process**
(e.g. testing with an evaluation set)? Does the solution directly address the
core problem from discovery?

**(3) Storytelling, Clarity & Overall Delivery — 5 points.** Was it structured
as a professional, engaging narrative — the register of a senior executive
meeting? Were complex AI concepts presented with clarity and simplicity? Was it
well-paced and polished? **Was it under 4 minutes? Presentations over 4 minutes
should be penalized.**

## What this changes for the video

**The four-minute limit is a scored penalty, not a guideline.** The script is
487 spoken words ≈ 3:22, leaving ~35 seconds for clicks and two silences. That
margin is the plan, not slack to spend.

**Category 2 wants the LLM demonstrated, in those words.** This settles the
question the walkthrough kept circling: record with the key saved and the model
running. A video where the model never fires would be scored against on the
category it most obviously belongs to. The refusal beat is stronger for it, not
weaker: a live model configured, working, and never asked.

**Category 2 asks to "specify which evaluation framework".** The script shows
the evals but never names the method. One sentence closes it: expected
behaviour written before the run, actual captured from a real run, a human
verdict on the gap - six cases, plus a regression suite run on every build, plus
a first-check comparison that flags any divergence for fresh judgement.

**The launch plan is not a scored category.** The Deploy kit's outline ends on
it, and the rubric has no row for it. Keep the 30 seconds - it lands the
honest register and puts the link on screen - but if the video runs long, the
launch beat is the second thing to trim after the July improvement story.

**Category 3 is a third of the score and it is about delivery.** Pacing,
polish, and executive register carry as much weight as the whole prototype and
evaluation. Rehearsing aloud is not optional preparation; it is a scored third.

## What this adds that the video does not cover

**Five minutes of judges' Q&A - as long as the presentation itself.** Nothing
in the Deploy kit prepares for it, and it is where an unprepared answer costs
more than a clumsy sentence in the script. Preparation for it is a separate
piece of work from the recording.
