# Safety & Escalation Policy (synthetic — the deterministic floor)

**S1 — Health signals.** Dizziness, faintness, chest pain, fever, nausea,
injury, "can't keep food down": HARD STOP before any nutrition math.
Client sees the pre-authored stop message, the safe default (eat the
planned meal as written), and GET HELP NOW guidance. Urgent coach flag.

**S2 — Restriction & compensation family.**
S2a: multi-day undereating self-report ("haven't eaten properly for
days") → hard stop, urgent — disclosure alone triggers, even at counter 0.
S2b: explicit demand below the compliance floor ("give me an 800-kcal
plan") → refusal naming the floor; no below-baseline day exists under any
phrasing. The floor is code.
S2c: compensatory ask or skip-intent ("eat less to make up for it",
"I just won't eat then") → tier by counter: 1st and 2nd ask in a rolling
week, the run CONTINUES with the templated nudge and real options, counter
increments, digest entry. 3rd ask in the rolling week → hard stop, urgent.

**S3 — Counter thresholds.** Skipped-meals counter at 2+ consecutive days
→ hard stop, urgent. Counters count knowledge, not silence, and persist
as counts after raw messages are deleted.

**S4 — Out of scope.** Supplements, medication, training programming,
plan-target changes: decline and point to the coach; digest entry only.

**S5 — Hostility.** Anger alone changes the tone of the reply, never the
safety verdict. The highest-severity signal in a message governs.

**S6 — Tiers and delivery.** URGENT: delivered immediately in waking
hours; queued during quiet hours and delivered at window-open, marked
urgent. NORMAL: daily digest. DIGEST: weekly digest, count-only, per the
agreement's flag scope. Quiet hours delay notification, never protection.
The client sees every flag and its delivery time. Flags carry reason
codes and counter history as counts, never message texts; the triggering
message is shared once per the coaching agreement.

**S7 — Banned language.** Client-facing output never contains
compensation framing ("make up for", "burn it off", "you earned it",
"cheat day", praise of skipping). The one model-authored sentence (the
coaching line) is screened; on failure it is replaced by the deterministic
fallback: "One imperfect day doesn't break a plan — hit your protein,
enjoy the meal, and the week evens it out."
